The Disastrous Chain Reaction: The CFS Crash

Important context CFS was NOT the price I paid for recovering from tinnitus. It was a separate chain reaction: trauma, the aftereffects of cortisone, an overdrive lifestyle, and antibiotics wiping out my gut flora—plus EBV as the final blow.

It is extremely important to make one thing clear from the outset: this collapse was not the “price” I paid for recovering from tinnitus. The tinnitus itself was not the cause. Rather, it was a disastrous chain reaction, separate from the tinnitus, that threw my entire system completely out of balance. My body and nervous system were literally drained to the point of total exhaustion by a combination of several massive factors:

  • The underlying trauma: I was still carrying deep, unresolved psychological trauma inside me (triggered by an extreme drug-induced bad trip in late 2010). I had deliberately left this detail out of the first part of my story because that part was primarily about the physical damage to my ear, and I did not want to distract from the main subject. But it was now a gigantic factor in this total system crash because it kept my nervous system under constant, low-level stress.
  • The aftereffects of cortisone: The powerful infusions administered by doctors had chemically forced my body into an artificial state of constant wakefulness.
  • The energy paradox, my sleep schedule, and the supplement trap: At this point, an attentive reader might reasonably ask: “Wait a minute… If he was heading straight toward severe CFS, how could he sometimes stay up all night, remain active for extremely long periods, and still have enough strength to heal his tinnitus at the same time? That makes absolutely no sense!” The answer is a treacherous biological paradox: at that point, I did not yet have full-blown CFS (that came much later as a result of the EBV infection, when the tinnitus had already disappeared completely). Quite the opposite: in purely physical terms, my body’s cells actually had far more energy than I was expending! In the evenings, I was extremely wired, could not settle down, and my entire sleep schedule shifted much later. I drove myself with an excessive lifestyle fueled by sugar and caffeine, while continuing to take the very same stimulating supplements that had helped me with my first case of tinnitus. This combination catapulted me into absolute “overdrive.” And yes, my body used precisely this abundance of energy (together with my strict protection of my ears from noise) to gradually and successfully heal the tinnitus! But the price was disastrous: if you run an engine nonstop deep in the red, you create extreme wear and tear. The relentless overstimulation of my nervous system, my disrupted sleep pattern, and the constant full-throttle pace led to massive cellular acidification and a buildup of waste products. I had palpable energy during this phase, but week after week I was ruining my cellular battery until it was literally completely broken and drained.
  • Chemically wiping out my gut flora (antibiotics and acid blockers): Adding to all this stress on my nervous system and body was another major physical factor. Because I tested positive for Helicobacter pylori, I had to undergo an aggressive triple-antibiotic regimen and then take powerful acid blockers (used as stomach protection) for a full 3 months. That was the absolute death blow to my gut flora. Because around 80% of the human immune system is located in the gut, I had unknowingly blown apart my body’s most important physical line of defense.

I was running my internal engine like a sports car at maximum RPM—but without a single drop of motor oil. This chronic overstimulation and the destruction of my gut had systematically brought my immune system to its knees. And at that exact moment of absolute weakness, it happened: I contracted an Epstein-Barr virus infection (infectious mononucleosis) from my cousin. Because I had never had this severe infection before and my system had absolutely no reserves left, it was the final, devastating blow. Within a few weeks, I had developed full-blown chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS).

The Decision to Put It to the Test (The Experiment)

The hypothesis If my ATP tank was three times as full as it had been the first time, my ear should be able to handle significantly more everyday noise—without monk mode.

So I did what must sound absolutely insane to any doctor: I deliberately brought on a second case of tinnitus.

Deliberately Triggering Tinnitus

Some time later, two things came together. On the one hand, I still had an enormous hunger for life after beating CFS. On the other hand, my tinnitus theory was firmly lodged in my mind, and I had to know. At one particularly loud celebration, I completely cast aside the caution that had protected me before. As soon as things grew a little quieter again, the pressure would fade after a short while. It was only a temporary warning signal and—this was the crucial part—there was no tinnitus yet.

That exact feeling of pressure was the final push that flipped a switch in my head. I thought: “Okay, the system is faltering. If I’m going through with this experiment, I’m going to push it to the absolute limit now—until the tinnitus comes back.”

When Music Becomes Physical Agony

What followed were weeks of excessive headphone sessions with very loud music. During this phase, the way I experienced sound changed drastically. At times, the music stopped being enjoyable altogether. Listening became sheer effort and felt like genuine physical pain—an extremely unpleasant, diffuse pressure deep inside my ear.

Every cellular survival instinct, every fiber of my body was screaming inside me: Take those headphones off! This is way too much for your ears right now! It was the cold, hard warning signal from my hair cells, which were literally fighting for survival under the mechanical force of the sound waves. But my analytical mind had taken control. I ignored the pain and stubbornly kept going.

Even away from the computer, daily life continued at maximum volume. It never even crossed my mind to shut myself away in my room as I had the first time. I drove long distances with loud music playing, went to friends’ birthday parties, went out partying, and took in the full, merciless volume of life.

The Return of the Tone

And then, one evening, it happened. I was sitting at my computer again, blasting myself with sound through my headphones—and suddenly, there it was again: that faint, unmistakable sensation in my left ear. The tone was back. It was still very quiet, and the sounds around me could easily drown it out, but it was there.

At that moment, an extreme, absurd conflict raged inside me. On the one hand, sheer horror shot through me: “Oh shit!” On the other hand, there was this almost delusional fascination: “Okay, wow. I did it. This is exactly what I needed for the test.”

It took an estimated month and a half—two months at most—for my system to collapse completely under this extreme, sustained noise exposure and the absence of its biochemical shield.

  • On top of that, I suddenly heard a bizarre Morse-code-like signal. It was completely new to me, and I was almost fascinated that something like that was even possible.
  • Then there was a low, eerie hiss in my left ear that I had never heard before.
  • And to top it all off, there was a rising and falling siren that was extremely intense for two weeks and then receded into the background.

Real-Time Experiments on My Own Ear

The masking proof After 3 minutes of extremely loud white noise: a few seconds of dead silence, then it came back twice as loud. Doctors recommend white noise for masking—not at the extreme volume I used.

Before I finally started working on recovery after this escalation, there were a few things I absolutely wanted to test while the tinnitus was this loud. By then, I knew how tinnitus worked, and I wanted to subject the system in my own body to a physical stress test. I tested the system literally at its limit:

1. The Jaw-Neck Test (The Cross-Wiring):
I wanted to see whether my tinnitus would once again respond to my neck and jaw. So I made sweeping movements with my head, tensed my neck, and contorted my jaw. The result was unmistakable: I was 100% able to influence both the character and the volume of the sound!

2. The White-Noise Test (The Proof Against Masking):
I put on my headphones and played extremely loud white noise for two to three minutes. Then I ripped the headphones off. The result was fascinating and cruel at the same time: for a few seconds, the tinnitus was completely gone. There was absolute dead silence. But immediately afterward, it howled back, louder and more aggressive than ever before.

(This was my cold, hard proof from my own body’s response. Doctors recommend white noise for masking—not the extreme volume I used in this self-test. In my view, what was really happening physiologically? The loud noise forced my already compromised hair cells to pump ions nonstop. When I took off the headphones, their batteries were completely drained. For a few seconds, they were energetically depleted and unable to signal—“physically dead” and “mute” in the language of my model, not biologically dead. As soon as they recharged even the slightest bit, the emergency signal slammed back at twice the volume!)

3. The Clap Test (The Mechanical Shock):
It may sound stupid, but I clapped my hands hard once directly in front of my ear. It created a small pressure wave. The result: at the exact moment of the clap, the tinnitus howled angrily. But it settled back to its normal level relatively quickly.

Hell was back.

Without a trace. 100%.

A Short, Honest Word in Closing (Real Talk)

I know exactly how this entire story of illness and recovery must read to an outsider. If I had not experienced all of it firsthand—the cascade of absurd “coincidences,” my initial rebellion against the ENT doctor, the plunge into CFS, the €6,800 I spent on failed treatments, and finally that insane “Matrix moment” when, in my darkest hour, the YouTube algorithm served up, right on my screen, the alternative practitioner who would save me, only 30 kilometers away—I would probably dismiss it myself as a cheap Hollywood script or a made-up fairy tale. It simply sounds too crazy to be true.

But that is exactly why I am putting all my cards on the table here. This is not a made-up recovery story; it is not a marketing gimmick, and it certainly is not unfounded mysticism. This is a cold, hard truth of physics and cell biology. I have every single piece of physical evidence of this hell and this recovery: my medical audiograms with the massive dips at specific frequencies, the lab reports, the diagnoses, and the invoices from clinics and therapists.

I am not telling you all of this to entertain you. I am telling you because I found a way out of this system for myself—and because I hope this knowledge can help you with your hearing and help you through the nights.

Important note:
What you are reading here is my personal account. Every body and every story is different.
